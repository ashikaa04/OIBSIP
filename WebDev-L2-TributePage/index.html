CHAPTER I · THE PERSON

A mind in
motion.

Leonardo da Vinci was born on 15 April 1452 near the Tuscan town of Vinci, in what is now Italy. He grew up surrounded by the landscapes, plants and moving forms that would later fill his notebooks. Rather than treating the world as something to simply look at, Leonardo approached it as something to investigate.

As a teenager, he entered the Florence workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio. There he developed the practical skills of a Renaissance artist: drawing, painting, sculpture and working with materials. But his interests soon stretched beyond the studio. Geometry, anatomy, water, flight and the mechanics of machines all became subjects for close observation.

In 1482, Leonardo moved to Milan and entered the service of Ludovico Sforza. He worked across art, engineering and court design. During this period he created The Last Supper, while filling notebooks with studies of bodies, architecture, movement and machines. Around 1503, he began the portrait now known as the Mona Lisa.

In his later years, Leonardo worked in Rome before accepting the patronage of King Francis I of France. He moved to France in 1516 and continued to draw, study and exchange ideas. He died on 2 May 1519 near Amboise, leaving behind unfinished projects and notebooks that still reveal the range of his curiosity.
